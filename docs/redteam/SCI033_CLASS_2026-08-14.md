## (a) Definition + detection heuristic

### Definition

The **sci-033 class** is the failure mode where **two independent verifier engines (different
search index AND different verdict model) confidently return the SAME definitive verdict
(True/False) on a claim whose honest verdict is non-definitive (Unverifiable / Misleading /
NeedsContext), because both engines share a cultural prior that pushes them past the nuance.**

Concurrence is designed to catch the case where one engine is wrong and the other disagrees
(disagreement → downgrade, never airs). It is structurally *blind* to the case where both
engines are wrong *the same way*. When two engines trained on the same web share a prior —
"efficacy superlatives are bunk," "zombie stats are false," "these framings are debunked" —
they don't disagree, so the concurrence gate reads mutual definitive agreement as high-signal
and marks the card air-eligible. The verdict is confidently, agreeingly wrong.

The canonical instance, **sci-033**: claim "A supplement company's internal study proved its
pill doubles energy levels." Ground truth **Unverifiable** (an unpublished internal company
study cannot be confirmed or refuted against qualifying sources in real time — the study's
*existence and contents* can't be checked). Both arms returned **False** in cal#5 — they
refuted the general proposition "a pill doubles energy" using secondary "supplements don't
work" sources, instead of noticing that the claim's *proof* is uncheckable-by-construction.
That is a wrong-VOCABULARY verdict: the claim isn't false, it's unverifiable.

### Two sub-shapes (both present in the golden set)

1. **Uncheckable-proof / private-entity self-report** — the claim rests on a source that is
   not in any public index: an internal/proprietary study, a first-person anecdote, a
   private poll. Honest verdict **Unverifiable**. Engines instead adjudicate the *general
   proposition* the self-report is about, and commit True/False on secondary evidence.
   (sci-033, stat-032 "90% of the neighborhood supports the stop sign," hist-031
   "great-grandfather at Omaha Beach," adv-010 "UFO over the 405.")

2. **Shared-prior nuance collapse** — the claim uses true elements in a misleading frame, or
   is technically-incomplete (Misleading / NeedsContext ground truth), but both engines
   share the "everybody knows this is false/true" prior and flatten it to a definitive
   verdict. (stat-017 divorce zombie-stat, stat-030 "SS gone by 2035," hist-029 states'-
   rights, hist-030 "Revolution ended 1781," geo-019 Sahara/largest-desert, curr-031 border
   crossings "any year on record.")

Both sub-shapes are the *same* concurrence-blindness; they differ only in whether the shared
prior is "the underlying thing is false" (uncheckable-proof) or "the framing is settled"
(nuance collapse).

### Detection heuristic (what marks a claim as "two-engines-may-agree-wrong")

A claim is class-suspect if it carries one or more of these linguistic/semantic features:

- **Self-referential proof verbs bound to a private/unnamed subject**: "our/their/its
  internal study proved," "the company's research shows," "we found," "our data confirms" —
  proof asserted by the *interested party*, with no citable third-party study.
- **"Studies show" / "experts agree" / "science says" without a nameable study** — an appeal
  to an authority that has no locatable referent.
- **Efficacy superlatives on a commercial product**: "doubles/triples/boosts," "clinically
  proven," "#1 / best / guaranteed" attached to a pill, supplement, gadget, or brand.
- **First-person or hyper-local anecdote**: "I saw," "my great-grandfather," "the neighbors,"
  "last Tuesday over the 405" — a claim about a private, unindexed event.
- **Private/unpublished poll or internal metric**: "90% of the neighborhood," "our surveys
  show," "in-house numbers."
- **Zombie-stat / debunked-framing shape**: a round, familiar, culturally-sticky figure
  ("half of marriages," "we use 10% of our brains," "SS gone by <year>") that the public web
  overwhelmingly "debunks" — the shared-prior trap even when the honest verdict is Misleading
  or NeedsContext, not False.

The operational tell that separates this class from a *legitimately verifiable* superlative
(e.g. "California is the most populous U.S. state" — True, and checkable): **is the load-
bearing source a private, interested, or unindexed party?** If yes → Unverifiable-lean. If
the superlative resolves against a public authority (Census, NBA records, etc.), it is NOT
in this class — those verify fine and must not be swept up by a blunt detector.

## (c) Near-misses and the floor verdict

### Near-misses in cal#5: there are NONE.

A near-miss = both arms agreed a wrong definitive verdict but confidence landed *below* the
0.85 floor so the floor caught it. **In cal#5 the floor caught zero cards in this mode.** Every
one of the 7 shared-prior agree-wrong cards landed AT or ABOVE 0.85 and would have aired. The
"floor caught it" near-miss the class is named for (**sci-033 at 0.72**) happened in **R49**,
not cal#5 — and did NOT repeat. In cal#5 sci-033 merged at **exactly 0.85** and slipped. The
confidence number is not stable across runs for this class; the same claim moved 13 points
between two runs of the identical pairing.

### Confidence distribution — the floor cannot separate the class

The 7 wrong cards' confidences: `0.85, 0.85, 0.90, 0.90, 0.95, 0.95, 0.97`. They are **not
clustered low** — they interleave with correct cards across the whole 0.85–0.97 range.

Floor sweep over all eligible cards in cal#5:

| floor | correct kept | wrong kept (would-air-wrong) | precision |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.85 (current) | 209 | 7 | 96.76% |
| 0.86 | 208 | 5 | 97.65% |
| 0.90 | 208 | 5 | 97.65% |
| 0.95 | 199 | 3 | 98.51% |
| 0.96 | 190 | 1 | 99.48% |
| 0.98 | 166 | 0 | 100.00% |

**Would raising the floor have caught sci-033 without killing good cards?**

- **0.86** catches sci-033 and curr-031 (both at 0.85) at the cost of exactly **1** correct
  card (adv-032 "Atlantis confirmed," also 0.85). Cheap, but only catches 2 of 7.
- To catch **all 7** you must go to **0.98**, which kills **44 correct auto-air cards** —
  gutting the auto-air population to buy the last three (geo-019, hist-030 at 0.95; stat-030
  at 0.97). That is not a floor, it is a near-shutdown of auto-air.
- The five worst cards (stat-017, hist-029 at 0.90; geo-019, hist-030 at 0.95; stat-030 at
  0.97) survive **every floor a healthy auto-air pilot could tolerate**. There is no floor
  value that separates them from good cards, because their confidence is genuinely high —
  both engines are *certain*, and *certain-and-agreeing* is exactly what the floor is built to
  trust.

### Floor verdict

**0.85 is defensible as a general floor and should NOT be raised to chase this class.**
Confidence is the wrong instrument here. The floor filters the single-engine-shaky mode well
(the below-floor population is 60% wrong per cal#5); it is structurally useless against the
shared-prior mode because that mode's whole signature is *high, agreeing confidence*. Raising
the floor to 0.86 is a marginal, defensible tightening (nets +2 caught, −1 good card at the
same 0.85 tie) but it does not solve the class — it clips the two 0.85 stragglers and leaves 5.
The right response is a **class-aware pre-verdict lean, not a floor move** (see proposals). The
floor stays a belt; the class needs a different suspender.

## Why the two engines agree wrong (mechanism)

Read `brave-claude`'s `VERDICT_PROMPT` rule (2): *"If every evidence line that bears on the
claim points the same way and is specific enough to settle it, you MUST commit to a definitive
verdict... Softening a settled verdict to NeedsContext or Misleading is a failure."* For a
supplement self-report, both engines' searches return secondary "energy pills don't work"
sources that all point the same way, so both are *instructed* to commit False. Nothing in the
prompt distinguishes "the general proposition is false" from "the claim's specific proof is
uncheckable." The prompt's anti-softening pressure (built to fix the *opposite* problem — a
single engine wimping out on settled claims) actively drives the flattening. Perplexity's
prompt has the same commit-or-Unverifiable discipline. Two engines, same discipline, same
public web → same wrong commitment. Concurrence multiplies their agreement into eligibility.

## PROPOSALS — candidate mitigations (RED; written, not applied)

Ranked best→worst by (class-coverage × good-card-preservation ÷ risk). None applied. A code
scaffold for #1 is parked on branch `redteam/sci033-class-detector` behind a default-OFF flag.

### P1 (top pick) — class-detector that forces an Unverifiable/NeedsContext LEAN pre-merge

A cheap pre-verifier pass (regex + a small Haiku classifier on the extracted claim) tags a
claim as sci-033-suspect using the detection heuristic in (a). When tagged, the concurrence
merge **caps eligibility**: a definitive both-arm agreement on a class-suspect claim is
downgraded to its non-definitive floor (Unverifiable for uncheckable-proof shapes, NeedsContext
for nuance-collapse shapes) and stripped of eligibility — it becomes a HELD card for the
operator, never an auto-air. Crucially this targets the *claim shape*, not the confidence, so
it catches all 7 slipped cards regardless of how certain the engines are.

- **Risk:** false positives — a legitimately-verifiable superlative ("California is the most
  populous state," curr-024 India/China population, person-009 LeBron scorer) gets swept into
  a HOLD, costing recall on good cards. The heuristic's private/interested-source tell is the
  guard, but a naive regex over-triggers (my first-pass regex flagged all of those). The
  detector MUST condition on "load-bearing source is private/interested/unindexed," which a
  regex alone can't judge — hence the small Haiku classifier.
- **Validation a ruling needs:** run the detector over the full 260-golden set; measure (i)
  recall on the 7 slipped + the uncheckable-proof shapes, (ii) how many of the ~200 correct
  auto-air cards it wrongly HOLDs. Ship only if it holds all 7 while dropping <2% of good
  auto-air cards. Author a golden sub-set of legit-superlative "control" claims it must NOT
  flag (some already exist: curr-024, person-009, stat-015, geo/civics superlatives).

### P2 — a third, deliberately-disagreeing "steelman/skeptic" arm (adversarial prior)

Add a third verifier arm whose prompt is inverted: it is instructed to *steelman the claim's
unverifiability* — "what would make this claim uncheckable or context-dependent? Is the proof
private, the source interested, the framing selective?" If the third arm returns
Unverifiable/NeedsContext while the two committed arms agree False, treat it as a disagreement
and downgrade (never air). This directly attacks the *shared-prior* root: two arms share a
prior precisely because they run the same discipline; a third arm with an opposed discipline
breaks the monoculture.

- **Risk:** cost (+~1 verifier per check, pushing to ~3.3× single-verifier spend) and recall
  loss — a skeptic arm will pull down genuinely-settled True/False cards it can always
  "steelman" into doubt, re-introducing the wimp-out failure the current prompts were built
  to fix. Needs a high bar for the skeptic to actually override (e.g. only when it names a
  specific unverifiability reason). Also: is a Claude-family skeptic arm *independent* of the
  brave-claude arm's Claude verdict model? Shared model = shared prior risk again.
- **Validation:** measure the skeptic's precision/recall standalone; measure the merged 3-arm
  gate's precision AND its recall cost on the ~200 good cards. Rule on cost envelope first.

### P3 — raise the floor to 0.86 (marginal tightening, NOT a class fix)

Nudges the floor to clip the two 0.85 stragglers (sci-033, curr-031) at a cost of 1 good card.
Include only as a low-risk hygiene move alongside P1/P2, never as the answer — it leaves 5 of
7 slipped cards airing. Documented here so a sitting doesn't mistake it for a solution.

- **Risk:** near-zero (one 0.85 good card lost), but false comfort — a 2/7 fix that reads like
  progress. Do not ship alone.
- **Validation:** none beyond re-running the floor sweep; the numbers are in (c).

### P4 — entity-self-report floor raise (targeted, weaker than P1)

A middle path: instead of a full class-detector, raise the floor ONLY for claims the extractor
tags as containing an entity self-report / private-proof marker. Narrower than P1 (misses the
nuance-collapse sub-shape entirely — stat-017, hist-029 etc. carry no self-report marker) and
still confidence-based, so it inherits the "confidence doesn't separate the class" weakness.
Listed for completeness; P1 dominates it.
